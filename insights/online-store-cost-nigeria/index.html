How much does an online store cost in Nigeria in 2026?

What you'll pay to build it, what you'll keep paying every month, and how to read a quote so you don't pay twice.

Johnson OjoFounder, SmilesPay Solutions Ltd · 18 November 2026 · 6 min read
Quick answer

Most Nigerian businesses should budget roughly ₦450,000 to ₦1,500,000 to have a proper online store built, plus about ₦150,000 to ₦400,000 a year to keep it running. Payment gateway fees come on top, per sale. A store built yourself on a website builder costs far less upfront, and a fully custom store with integrations can run into several million naira.

"How much is a website?" is one of the most common questions business owners ask, and the honest answer is that "online store" covers very different things. A WhatsApp-style catalogue with a pay button and a store that syncs stock, calculates delivery by area and settles payments into your business account are both online stores. The difference is what each one can do for your business.

3. Payment gateway fees, explained

Paystack, one of the most widely used gateways in Nigeria, publishes its fees openly. For local payments it charges 1.5% + ₦100 per transaction. The ₦100 is waived on payments under ₦2,500, and the total local fee is capped at ₦2,000. International cards cost more: 3.9% + ₦100. Other gateways price in a similar range, so compare them on your typical order size, not the headline rate.

4. What actually drives the price up

  • Number of products and variations. Fifty products in three sizes is a different job from 2,000 products in many colours.
  • Delivery logic. Flat delivery fees are simple. Fees by Lagos area, by state or by weight take more work, and save you money on every order.
  • Payment options. Card, transfer, USSD and pay-on-delivery each need handling, especially failed or abandoned payments.
  • Inventory. If you also sell in a physical shop or on Instagram, stock has to stay in sync or you'll sell items you don't have.
  • Custom design. A template is cheaper. A design built around your brand converts better.
  • Admin features. Staff accounts, order statuses, refunds and reports are what make the store run without you.

5. How to avoid paying twice

A store that was cheap to build can be expensive to own, and many end up being rebuilt from scratch. Before you pay anyone, make sure:

  1. You own the domain, hosting and code. Get the logins and the source code in writing.
  2. The payment account is in your business name, so settlements go straight to you.
  3. Backups run automatically and you know how to restore one.
  4. There's a handover document explaining how the store works, so another developer can pick it up.
  5. The quote lists what's included and what isn't, including running costs and a support window after launch.

Questions to ask before you accept a quote

  • Is this a template or built for my business? Can I see a store you've built?
  • What happens if a customer pays and the order doesn't show up?
  • How are delivery fees calculated?
  • Who fixes things after launch, how fast, and at what cost?
  • Will I get the source code and all the logins?
